Before work begins

Match the wiring to how the room is now used.

Picture a home with a dedicated office, studio or hobby room. Its equipment may sit together in one corner on a single circuit, and adding a kiln or a second workstation changes the load that circuit carries.

Before anyone visits, write down what the room did before, what it will do now and which devices run together. Photograph the outlets and light positions without removing any cover, so the contractor sees the change you describe.

Which records should I gather when a room changes purpose?

Gather earlier ESA paperwork, any drawings, receipts for past work and the equipment sheets for what you plan to add. Note who in the household decides on the room's new use, so the contractor has one person to ask.

Should the new workstation share a circuit with the lights?

That is a question for the contractor, not a guess. Ask whether the lights, the computer gear and any heater or kiln are likely to run together, and have the answer written into the scope.

How do I compare quotes when each one assumes a different room use?

Give every contractor the same written description of how the room is used now and how it will be used. Quotes built on the same description can then be compared line by line.

Who decides whether a room change needs approval beyond ESA?

The municipality decides about building permissions, and ESA covers the electrical notification. The two are separate, so ask the contractor which one your plan touches and ask the municipality directly about the other.

Does sending my enquiry about a changed room arrange a visit?

No. The site is for reading and looks over each enquiry it receives; it takes no part in visits, pricing or electrical work. If you go on with a Licensed Electrical Contractor, it first confirms its name and licence, then agrees the work and its cost with you directly. Dates come last, and any visit is confirmed separately.
